## 1. Short answer

- **Original purpose:** a kit plus a free hosted site for small browser multiplayer games: rooms with a
  code and password, a lobby with teams, bots that join like players, and a browser client, so
  "every new idea starts at the fun part". [Checked: first README, `6ca85ae`]
- **What exists:** a working alpha. 142 of 142 tests pass on `main`. One game (Control Points, 10 v 10
  with bots) runs from a GitHub link. The distinctive part is hosting strangers' games by link with the
  server never running their code. [Checked: my test run and `README.md`, `d157dd9`]
- **The earlier report mostly holds up.** Its numbers are right. Three of its privacy points are
  overstated or wrong (section 4), and two of its "questions only you can answer" were already answered
  the day before it was written.
- **The repo's own to-do list is out of date** in at least three places (section 5).
- **Before it can be shown:** a public-facing tidy (section 6), a fix for the warning banner that tells
  visitors nobody has played Josh's own demo, and a decision on three public side branches. None of that
  is big. What is big, and can't be done by 4 November, is real players and a way to count them.
- **The biggest open conflict** is direction, not code: there are three different near-term targets
  for friendslop on record (section 5, item 8).

## 2. Original purpose, and how it moved

1. **25 Oct, first README:** "A kit for vibecoding small, chaotic multiplayer games and playing them
   with your friends tonight." Rooms with a code and password, a host-run lobby, snap-in game modes,
   bots that join like players, **"Host from your own PC"**, runs in a browser. First modes planned:
   capture the flag in first person, and control points. Licence undecided. [Checked: `README.md` at
   `6ca85ae`]
2. **26 Oct, design spec:** the target user is someone who has "vibecoded a small multiplayer game and
   wants their friends playing it tonight", for whom hosting is the obstacle. Goal: paste one GitHub
   link, get a room link, free, without Josh doing anything; Josh reviews games later for a listing.
   [Checked: `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-26-game-hosting-design.md`, `d157dd9`]
3. **What changed:** "host from your own PC" became one central free server (play.friendslop.us)
   carrying every room. Capture the flag was never built; control points was. The licence became
   Apache-2.0. [Checked: `README.md`, `render.yaml`, `LICENSE`, `d157dd9`]
4. **28 Oct onward, Josh's own concern:** the site only looks easy to host on because it carries
   everyone's hosting on a free plan; he asked for a way to make hosting really easy for others.
   [Checked: `TODO.md` "Hosting: who carries it", `d157dd9`]
5. **31 Oct to 1 Nov, scope widened:** retro games in lockstep emulators, games from a PC launcher,
   modded commercial games, and Josh's thesis that "getting a group of friends into the same game at
   the same time" is the bottleneck. These are notes and a spike, not shipped features. [Checked:
   `docs/research/2026-10-31-*.md`, `docs/research/2026-11-01-modded-games-direction.md`, `d157dd9`]

The brief's description ("open the site, get a room code, friends join in a browser tab... a 10 v 10
low-poly shooter with bots ships as the demo") matches the README. One nuance: the public server has
**no games installed** (`GAMES_DIR` is an empty folder); the demo itself is loaded by link from this
repo's `main` through a public CDN, like any stranger's game. [Checked: `render.yaml`,
`client/js/screens/home.js` line 16, `d157dd9`]
